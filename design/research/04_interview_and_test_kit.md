# SkinKey field kit: shopper interviews and share-flow test

**For:** team members who are not researchers. Run it on a phone, in English, in a café, college or office.
**Today:** 1 Nov 2026. **Submission deadline:** 2 Nov 2026, 11:45 AM ET (evidence.md). That is 10:15 PM IST on 2 Nov. *(The IST time was worked out by the team and is not in our files. US clocks go back to EST on 1 Nov 2026.)*
**Each session:** about **12 minutes for the participant**: consent (1), interview part 1 (3), share-flow test (4), interview part 3 (3), close and quote ask (1). Allow about 15 minutes per session once you add screening and reset. **(ASSUMPTION)**
**Target:** 3 to 5 sessions in the next 1 to 2 hours, then one 15-minute debrief.

> **Read this first: what this kit can and cannot tell you**
> - 3 to 5 chats with people we found nearby is a small convenience sample, so it only points in a direction. Report results as counts, like **"3 of 5 people…"**. Never use percentages, and never write "Indian shoppers think…".
> - **ASSUMPTION (to validate)** marks a team guess with no evidence behind it. These sessions exist to test those guesses.
> - **The proto-personas (0.3) are hypotheses, not research findings.** Expect them to be wrong.
> - **Every statistic** in this kit comes from `/home/aman/youcam/research/evidence.md` or `/home/aman/youcam/research/skinkey_report.md`, with its source next to it. The procedural numbers (timings, hint seconds, thresholds, note counts) are team choices. Don't add statistics from memory.
> - These sessions test **share → verdict, the verdict cards, the selfie consent and the pitch**. They do **not** test Skin Forecast, Aisle Check, pharmacist QR/revoke or the makeup preview. Don't make any decision about those features from today.

## 1. (a) Recruiting and consent

### 1.1 Who to approach

- **Anyone who looks roughly 20–35, any gender.** The screener confirms age. Don't guess gender, don't set a gender quota, and don't ask about gender except with the optional question at the end.
- **Café:** people alone or in relaxed pairs. Avoid anyone on a call, eating with family, or in a hurry.
- **College:** canteen, library steps, common areas. **No one under 20.**
- **Office:** cafeteria and break areas. Colleagues and friends of the team are allowed, but tick **"knows team"**. Our guess (ASSUMPTION) is that they'll be kinder about the idea, so their rows are flagged in the tally.
- **Soft variety:** try for different main stores (Amazon, Flipkart, Nykaa), at least one person who doesn't follow creators, and at least one iPhone and one Android user.
- **Accept a "no" the first time.** Never follow anyone or ask twice. Log every approach in the tally.
- **Thank-you (ASSUMPTION):** if there's budget, offer a tea or coffee, and make clear it doesn't depend on their answers.

### 1.2 Opening line (say it word for word)

> "Hi, sorry to interrupt! We're a small team building an app for people who buy skincare online, for a hackathon. Could I ask two quick questions to see if you'd be up for a 12-minute chat? No selling, and no photos of you."

### 1.3 Screener (2 questions)

**S1.** "In the last 3 months, have you bought, or had someone buy for you, anything for your face or skin online? That could be face wash, moisturiser, serum, sunscreen, or beard or shaving products, on Amazon, Flipkart, Nykaa or any other app."
- **Yes** → "Which app?" Write it down. Go to S2.
- **No / not sure** → "No problem, thank you so much!" End.

**S2.** "Just to check, are you under 20, between 20 and 35, or over 35?"
- **20 to 35** → go to consent.
- **Otherwise** → "Thanks, we're talking to 20-to-35s today. Really appreciate it!" End.

Don't screen on these, but **note them**: works in beauty retail or at Amazon, Flipkart or Nykaa? Knows the team?

### 1.4 Consent (read word for word, then get a clear verbal "yes")

> "Thanks! Here's what this is:
> - About 10 minutes: some questions about how you buy skincare online, and a quick try of an app idea on **our** phone. Then one short, optional question at the end.
> - **We only take written notes.** No audio, no video, and **no photos of you or your face.** We won't scan your face today.
> - We won't write your name or number. You'll just be a code, like 'A1'.
> - You can **skip any question or stop at any time**, no reason needed.
> - There are no right or wrong answers. We're testing the app idea, not you.
> - We can't give skincare or medical advice.
> - Only our team sees the notes. We'll use them to improve the app and summarise them, without names, in our hackathon entry. We'll delete the notes by **[delete-by date from 0.1]**.
> - At the end we may ask if we can show one of your sentences, as text with no name, in our demo video. That's a separate yes or no, and 'no' is completely fine.
>
> Is that okay with you?"

- Note-taker ticks **Consent: verbal yes** and writes the time.
- Treat any hesitation as a no: "Totally fine, thanks anyway!"
- If they want to show their own phone: "You can just describe it if you'd rather. If you show us, we won't take screenshots, and please hide anything private."

## 2. (b) Interview guide, plus (c) the share-flow test, in run order

**Run order:** Part 1 (interview) → **Part 2 (share test)** → Part 3 (concept). The share test comes **before** the concept so the concept can't prime finding Share. One probe at most per question.

### Part 1: interview (0:00–3:00). Don't mention SkinKey

**W1.** "Which apps do you usually buy skincare or grooming things on? And is your phone Android or iPhone?"

**C1.** "Think about the **last** one you bought online. What was it, which app, and roughly what did it cost?"
- *Probe:* "Did the price decide it?"

**C2. (H1)** "What made you pick that one over the others?"
- *Probe:* "Where did you first hear about it: a friend, a creator, an ad, the app?"
- **Only if they bring up skin or fit:** "Do you think of yourself as having a skin type? Where did that come from?"

**D2. (H2)** "When you see words like niacinamide, retinol, AHA or BHA, or SPF numbers on a product, what goes through your mind?"
- *Probe:* "Which of these, if any, have you looked for or avoided? How did you decide?" ("None" is a real answer. Write it down.)

**D3. (H3)** "Has there been a skincare product that didn't work out, or that you didn't really use? It's fine if not."
- **Only if yes:** "What happened, and what did you do with it?"

### Part 2: share-flow test (3:00–7:00), section (c)

The participant holds the phone and does the tapping. The Facilitator only touches it to give a hint.

**Setup (20 s).** Hand over the team phone with the Flipkart product page open. Show the **profile card** (3.1):
> "Pretend you've already set up a skin app with a one-time selfie, and this is your skin profile."

**Neutral task (word for word):**
> "You're looking at this product and wondering if it would suit your skin. There's an app on this phone that can help with that. Show me what you'd do, and think out loud."

**Finding Share (up to 90 s).**
- Watch silently. Note-taker: **where they look first**, and a time band from the phone clock.
- At **30 s**, give Hint 1: "What else could you do on this screen?"
- At **60 s**, give Hint 2: "Is there a way to send this product to another app?"
- At **90 s**, point to Share.
- Record one of: **<30 s unassisted / 30–60 s unassisted / Hint 1 / Hint 2 / Shown.**
- If they open a different app (for example a browser or Google Lens), let them try for a moment. Record what they did. That's data.

**Expectation (15 s).** When the share sheet is open, **before they pick**, ask:
> "Before you choose anything, what do you expect to happen if you pick SkinKey here?"

(Nykaa-first shoppers only, 10 s: "If you were in the Nykaa app, where would you look to do this?")

**Show Card 1.**
- If the dev build is in the list, let them tap it.
- If it isn't (Wizard-of-Oz), say: "Imagine SkinKey is in this list. Where would you expect it to be? Tap there." Then open Card 1 in the gallery.
- With **Version B** cards, also say: "The app isn't finished, so this is a sample screen for a made-up product. Pretend it's what you'd see."
- Then: "Read it out loud or to yourself, whichever you prefer, and tell me what it says."

**Card rotation** (to balance order effects):

| Code ends in | Card 1 | Card 2 | Bonus (only if they offer an extra minute) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 3, 5 | CAUTION | UNKNOWN | Medicine card |
| 2, 4 | Based on product name only | UNKNOWN | FITS |

Note-taker records what they read first, what they skip, and whether they notice the reasons, the "Where this comes from" block and the "not medical advice" line.

**Comprehension questions.** Score each one **✓ / ~ / ✗**. Don't correct them. If unsure, both team members score it and agree after the session.

| # | Ask (word for word) | ✓ correct | ~ partly (example) | ✗ wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CQ1 | "In your own words, what is this screen telling you about the product?" | CAUTION: it may not suit them, be careful. Name-only: a weaker check, from the name only. | "It's some kind of warning", but they can't say about what | "It's dangerous / banned / fake", or "it's great" |
| CQ2 | "What is that based on?" | Links a product feature to a reading on **the profile card** (e.g. fragrance ↔ redness). Whether they agree with the rule is **not** scored. | Mentions only the product side or only the skin side | "Reviews", "ratings", "the store said so" |
| CQ3 | "Where do you think the information about your skin, and about the product, came from?" Then, unscored: "How much would you trust that, in your own words?" | Skin: the selfie scan (YouCam). Product: the ingredient list (CAUTION) or the product name only (name-only card). | Gets one of the two | Neither, or "Flipkart gave it" |
| CQ4 | *(Show Card 2, UNKNOWN.)* "What's different about this one? What would you do now?" | The app doesn't have enough information. It is **not** saying the product is bad. | "Not sure what it means" | Reads it as bad or unsafe |
| CQ5 | *Only if they never mentioned the disclaimer:* "What kind of advice is this screen giving you?" | Cosmetic, general, "not medical" | Vague | Medical or doctor-like advice |
| Bonus FITS | "What does FITS mean to you here?" | Matches their profile on what's known. Not a guarantee. | "Probably fine" | "It will definitely work" |
| Bonus medicine | "What is this telling you? Is it saying anything about your skin?" | The product looks like a medicine, so ask a pharmacist. It says nothing about their skin. | Unsure | Reads it as a diagnosis ("I have a condition") |

Don't ask any follow-up about their own skin problems or health.

**"Find better matches" (30 s).**
- Ask: "What do you expect happens if you tap **Find better matches**?" Then: "Would you expect those to be within your budget?" Write down their words.
- If the filter link from 0.1 works, open it in the app or in Chrome, whichever the prep check showed keeps the filter. Watch their reaction.
- Say "**Flipkart search, already filtered** to the sample profile's skin type". Don't say "your" skin type, and don't say "SkinKey is inside Flipkart".
- If the link isn't ready, skip opening it and keep only the expectation answer.

### Part 3: interview, concept (7:00–10:00)

**K1.** "Have you ever used anything that scans your face, or asks you skin questions, in a shopping app or anywhere else?"
- Note whether they mention a store tool **unprompted**.
- If they don't, ask (this counts as **prompted**): "Some shopping apps have one. Flipkart's app has had a selfie skin analyser, and Amazon has a skin quiz. Have you come across either?"

**Consent-screen choice (H4, behaviour proxy).** Show the consent-screen card (3.2):
> "This would be the first screen of the app. Nothing happens when you tap, and we won't take a photo. Which would you tap, and what would you want to know first?"

- Record **Start scan / Not now**, plus their first question.
- If **Not now:** "What would you use instead, if anything?" (For example a quiz, choosing your skin type yourself, or asking a pharmacist. Don't read these options out.)

**K2. Concept (read once, word for word, then stop talking):**
> "Here's the whole idea, called SkinKey. Once, if you agree, you take a selfie. It's sent to a skin-analysis service called YouCam, which measures things like oiliness, moisture and redness. SkinKey stores no photo: YouCam processes it and we delete it from YouCam right after. Only simple bands like 'high' or 'low' are kept on your phone. Then, from the Flipkart or Amazon app, you share a product to SkinKey and it says FITS, CAUTION or UNKNOWN, with reasons from your readings. Some limits: it only knows a small list of products, so for many it will say UNKNOWN or check from the product name only. Skin AI can be less accurate on deeper skin tones and in poor light. And 'Find better matches' opens a Flipkart search filtered to your skin type, or a keyword search on Amazon or Nykaa."

- "How do you feel about it?"
- Then: "Think of the [C1 product] you told me about. At what moment, if any, would this have come in?"

**K3. Skin tone and accuracy.** Point to the line "Skin AI can be less accurate on deeper skin tones…" on the profile card:
> "What do you make of this line? Has a skin tool, shade match or skin advice ever got your skin wrong?"

(Don't judge or record anyone's skin tone. Record only what they say.)

**K4 (H8). Only if they know a store's tool:**
> "If you had the store's own tool and this one, which would you use for the product in front of you, and why?"

If they don't know a store tool, record **"no basis to compare"**.

**K5 (H9).**
> "In the app, 'Find better matches' just opens the store's search. If SkinKey's **website** earned a small commission when you bought something through a link on it, how would you feel about that?"

**Close (10:00–11:00).**
- "Anything I should have asked but didn't?"
- **Optional:** "How would you describe your gender, if you'd like to say? Totally fine to skip." Write their words exactly, or "skipped".
- Then go to the quote ask (section 5).

## 5. (e) Capturing 1 or 2 usable quotes for the demo video, with permission

**Text-only, anonymous, on-screen quotes.** No faces, no audio, no voice. This matches the consent you read out.

1. **During the session:** the Note-taker writes candidate quotes **exactly as said**, in quotation marks, marked ★. If you're unsure of the exact words, mark it **[P]**. A paraphrase can never be used as a quote.
2. **At the close, ask (word for word):**
   > "One last thing, and 'no' is completely fine. You said: '*<read their exact words back>*'. Could we show that sentence, with no name, as text in a short public video for our hackathon entry? You can change the wording now if you like."
3. **Record the answer:**
   - **No:** thank them. That quote is never used.
   - **Yes:** ask how to credit it: no label, **"Shopper, 20s"** or **"Shopper, 30s"**. Never use a name, college, employer or exact location.
4. **Read back the final wording,** get a clear "yes", and log it:

| Code | Approved exact wording | Credit as | Time of yes | Witness (2nd team member) | Slip given |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

5. **Hand over the withdrawal slip** ("Your code: A1 · To withdraw your quote, contact [team contact] before [withdrawal deadline]"). If they withdraw, delete the quote everywhere.
6. **Video rules:**
   - Approved words only. Don't splice quotes together or trim them in a way that changes the meaning.
   - Label every quote on screen: *"Shopper, from an informal ~12-minute conversation, 1 Nov 2026 (one of n). Not a survey."*
   - **Statistics:** every on-screen statistic must come from `evidence.md` and carry its source and caveat. Example: "63% are wrong about their skin type (Labskin 2022, vendor survey; country not stated)". **Never put a statistic on the same screen as a participant quote**, so neither is read as typical of Indian shoppers.
   - Don't use quotes that mention a health condition or medicine, name anyone, or make claims about Flipkart's or Amazon's own tools. (The last one is a team judgment call to avoid brand and legal trouble.)
   - Don't use a quote to claim SkinKey "works", "treats" something or gives medical results. SkinKey gives cosmetic guidance only.
